est. in the north

Stories carried from yesterday, read today, planted for tomorrow.

Takarda is a book club and reading community rooted in Northern Nigeria — bringing together the region's oral past, its present-day readers, and the libraries we're building for the next generation.

Join our community Read the blog

takarda (n.) — Hausa for "paper"

A club built on the idea that a page is never just one moment — it's a thread from the storyteller who came before, through the reader holding it now, to the reader who hasn't been born yet.

Every month, we gather to read, argue gently about endings, and trade the books that shaped us. Every book we finish moves on — to a neighbour, a cousin, a village library — so nothing we read stays still for long.

how we hold time

Three eras, one shelf

Takarda was built around a simple structure — everything we do sits in one of three places on the timeline.

Past

The griots, the Ajami manuscripts, the folktales told over pounded millet — we read northern and West African classics alongside oral histories collected from elders in our own community, so the archive stays alive rather than sealed in a shelf.

Present

Monthly meetups, rotating between homes, courtyards and the occasional bookshop. We read contemporary Nigerian and African fiction, hold open-mic reading nights, and keep a running, arguable list of what's worth your evening.

Future

Through the Takarda Eco-Reading Project, every book we circulate funds a reading corner or a tree planted in its honour — building the libraries and the shade the next generation of readers will sit under.

who's behind takarda

Two founders, one strategist, and a growing club

Takarda started as two friends trading paperbacks after Jumu'ah prayers. It's since grown into a standing monthly club and a small team keeping the paperwork — and the storytelling — moving.

for the future

The Takarda Eco-Reading Project

Takarda means paper — and paper starts as a tree. For every reading corner we open, we plant trees in the same community, so the villages lending us their afternoons get shade and timber back in return. Your donation funds books, shelving, and seedlings in one line item.
